# Metric catalogue

Every metric this system can compute, with its formula and what it counts. These are the only names a query may use; anything else is rejected rather than approximated. No figures appear here — a metric's value is whatever the query returns today.

## `volume` — Volume (transaction count)

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions
- **Counts:** every attempt, whatever its outcome

The plain count of attempts, declines included. Use it for demand and reliability questions. It is not a money figure, and 'high volume' never means 'high revenue'.

## `value` — Value (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt`
- **Counts:** successful transactions (applied automatically unless the query names an outcome)
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure

The revenue figure. Declined attempts carry an amount but moved no money, so they are excluded; a sum that includes them overstates every total.

## `ats` — Average ticket size (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` ÷ count of transactions
- **Counts:** successful transactions (applied automatically unless the query names an outcome)
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure

Average ticket size: how large a typical successful transaction is. Undefined, not zero, for a group with no successful transactions.

## `spend_per_card` — Spend per card (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` ÷ distinct count of `t.card_id`
- **Counts:** successful transactions (applied automatically unless the query names an outcome)
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure

Value spread over the cards that transacted. Falls when a campaign brings in many lightly-used cards, which is not the same as customers spending less.

## `spend_per_customer` — Spend per customer (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` ÷ distinct count of `t.customer_id`
- **Counts:** successful transactions (applied automatically unless the query names an outcome)
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure

Value spread over customers who transacted. Compare with spend per card to see whether people hold several active cards.

## `active_cards` — Active cards

- **Calculation:** Distinct count of `t.card_id`
- **Counts:** every attempt, whatever its outcome

Cards that attempted at least one transaction in the period. Activity, not issuance.

## `active_card_rate` — Active card rate

- **Calculation:** `COUNT(DISTINCT t.card_id) / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM card_master)`
- **Counts:** every attempt, whatever its outcome
- **Range:** 0 to 1 — a fraction, so 10% is written 0.1
- **Note:** denominator = total rows in card_master

Active cards against every card ever issued, so the denominator includes cards that have never been used and does not shrink with the period. A short period therefore looks low by construction.

## `decline_rate` — Decline rate

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd <> 'success'` ÷ count of transactions
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD <> 'Success'`
- **Range:** 0 to 1 — a fraction, so 10% is written 0.1
- **Needs:** the response_master join
- **Note:** business + technical; use business_decline_rate or technical_decline_rate for one class

Every non-approval: business and technical together. This is what 'decline rate' means unqualified, and it can rank groups differently from either class alone.

## `decline_volume` — Declined volume

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd <> 'success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD <> 'Success'`
- **Needs:** the response_master join

All failed attempts, business and technical.

## `decline_value` — Declined value (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd <> 'success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD <> 'Success'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Money attempted but not captured. The size of the problem, which a rate never says.

## `decline_ats` — Declined average ticket size (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd <> 'success'` ÷ count of transactions where `r.TD_BD <> 'Success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD <> 'Success'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master, response_master join

Ticket size of failed attempts. Compare with success_ats: if declines are larger, failures are concentrated in high-ticket transactions and cost more than the rate suggests.

## `success_rate` — Success rate

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'success'` ÷ count of transactions
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Success'`
- **Range:** 0 to 1 — a fraction, so 10% is written 0.1
- **Needs:** the response_master join

The share of attempts approved. The headline health measure; its complement is the decline rate.

## `business_decline_rate` — Business decline rate

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'business decline'` ÷ count of transactions
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Business Decline'`
- **Range:** 0 to 1 — a fraction, so 10% is written 0.1
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Only issuer refusals about the account. Customer affordability, limits and risk policy; largely outside the payment organization's control.

## `technical_decline_rate` — Technical decline rate

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'technical decline'` ÷ count of transactions
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Technical Decline'`
- **Range:** 0 to 1 — a fraction, so 10% is written 0.1
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Only infrastructure failures. Usually actionable and usually recoverable, so a rise here is an incident rather than a trend.

## `success_value` — Successful value (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd = 'success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Success'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Value stated explicitly rather than implicitly, useful beside the declined columns.

## `success_volume` — Successful volume

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Success'`
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Approved attempts only, for comparison with the declined counts.

## `success_ats` — Successful average ticket size (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd = 'success'` ÷ count of transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Success'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Success'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master, response_master join

Ticket size of approved transactions.

## `business_decline_value` — Business-declined value (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd = 'business decline'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Business Decline'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Money lost to issuer refusals.

## `business_decline_volume` — Business-declined volume

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'business decline'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Business Decline'`
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Count of issuer refusals.

## `business_decline_ats` — Business-declined average ticket size (₹)

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd = 'business decline'` ÷ count of transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Business Decline'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Business Decline'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master, response_master join

Ticket size of issuer refusals; a high figure points at limit codes.

- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Money lost to system failures — the recoverable part of lost revenue.

- **Calculation:** Count of transactions where `r.td_bd = 'technical decline'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Technical Decline'`
- **Needs:** the response_master join

Count of system failures.

- **Calculation:** Sum of `t.amt` where `r.td_bd = 'technical decline'` ÷ count of transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Technical Decline'`
- **Counts:** transactions where `r.TD_BD = 'Technical Decline'`
- **Units:** money, so `IN LAKH` or `IN CRORE` rescales the displayed figure

Ticket size of system failures. Usually close to the overall ticket size, because infrastructure does not care how large a transaction is.

## Choosing between them

- **Money or count?** `value` and its variants are rupees; `volume` and its variants are counts. A question about revenue takes the first, a question about attempts or reliability the second.
- **Which outcome?** An unqualified money or count metric already means successful transactions. Name an outcome explicitly (`success_*`, `business_decline_*`, `technical_decline_*`, `decline_*`) when outcomes need to sit side by side in one table.
- **Rate or amount?** A rate says how often something happens; a value says what it is worth. Questions about impact need the amount, because a small rate on large transactions can cost more than a large rate on small ones.
- **Rates are fractions.** Every metric with a range of 0 to 1 is written that way in a query: 'under 10%' is `< 0.1`.
